Hawaii flight and hotel: which island, and when.

Hawaiʻi is a long flight from most of the mainland, and hotels are expensive, so the trip rewards planning. The biggest decision isn't the hotel but the island, because each one has its own airport, its own weather patterns and its own kind of stay.

When to go

Peak, shoulder and the whales.

Mid-December to March is high season: winter escapes, the holidays and humpback whale season, when whales are most often seen from January to March. Flights and hotels cost the most and the best resorts sell out early.

June to August is busy too, with family travel during school holidays.

Mid-April to early June and September to mid-December (outside Thanksgiving) are the shoulder seasons, usually with the best prices and good weather.

Weather depends more on which side of an island you're on than on the month. Leeward (west and south) coasts such as Waikīkī, Wailea and Kona are drier and sunnier; windward and northern coasts are greener and wetter. North shores get big surf in winter and calmer water in summer. The Central Pacific hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November, though direct hits on the islands are rare.

Where to stay

Four main islands, four different trips.

Oʻahu is the easiest first trip: Honolulu, Waikīkī's walkable beach, Pearl Harbor and the North Shore, with the most flights and hotels. See our sister guide on where to stay on Oʻahu.

Maui is the classic resort island: Wailea on the sunny south side, Kāʻanapali and Kapalua on the west side, plus the Road to Hāna and Haleakalā. More on where to stay on Maui.

Hawaiʻi Island (the Big Island) has the Kohala Coast resorts and Kona on the dry west side, Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park on the east, and long drives in between.

Kauaʻi is quieter and greener, with Poʻipū on the sunny south shore and Princeville on the north shore, and the Nā Pali Coast and Waimea Canyon to explore.

Getting there

Fly into the island you're staying on.

Each main island has its own airport: Honolulu (HNL) for Oʻahu, Kahului (OGG) for Maui, Kona (KOA) and Hilo (ITO) for the Big Island, and Līhuʻe (LIH) for Kauaʻi. Flying direct from the mainland to your island saves a connection in Honolulu and is often worth a little more.

Visiting two islands? Book the inter-island flight separately and allow a travel day. Outside Waikīkī you'll almost certainly want a rental car, and resorts often charge for parking, so add those costs when you compare.

Should you bundle?

Sometimes. Compare the total, fees included.

Packages can save money on Hawaiʻi trips because flights from the mainland are a big share of the cost and hotels sell discounted package rates. They work best for a one-island stay at one hotel.

Before you book, check what's not in the headline price. Many Hawaiʻi hotels add a daily resort fee and charge for parking, and state and county hotel taxes are added on top. Two packages with the same price can end up far apart once those are counted. For multi-island trips, it's often simpler to book the main island as a package and the second island separately.

Beyond the hotel

Plan the big days ahead.

Some of Hawaiʻi's best-known sights need advance planning. On Oʻahu, Hanauma Bay, the Diamond Head hike and the USS Arizona Memorial all use reservations (see Hanauma Bay, Diamond Head and Pearl Harbor guides). On Maui, watching sunrise from Haleakalā needs a National Park Service reservation. On the Big Island, Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park is a full day from the Kona side, and volcanic activity changes what you can see, so check the park's current conditions.

Questions

Short answers to common questions. For the month-by-month comparison with the other destinations, see the home page.

What is the cheapest time to fly to Hawaii?

Usually the shoulder seasons: mid-April to early June and September to mid-December, avoiding Thanksgiving. Mid-December to March and the summer school holidays are the busiest and most expensive times.

Which Hawaiian island is best for a first trip?

Oʻahu is the easiest: the most flights, a wide range of hotels, and Waikīkī is walkable without a car. Maui is the most popular choice for a resort-focused trip. The Big Island suits travelers who want volcanoes and variety, and Kauaʻi suits those who want a quieter, greener island.

Is a Hawaii flight and hotel package worth it?

It can be, especially for a one-island stay, because flights are a large part of the cost. Compare the package total with booking separately, and include daily resort fees, parking and hotel taxes, which can add a lot to Hawaiʻi stays.

Do I need a car in Hawaii?

On Oʻahu you can manage without one if you stay in Waikīkī and use buses, tours or rideshares. On Maui, the Big Island and Kauaʻi, a rental car is close to essential.
