Costa Rica packs volcanoes, cloud forest, two coastlines and a lot of wildlife into a small country, but roads are slow, so most trips are either one beach base or a loop through two or three regions. That choice decides your airport, your hotels and whether a package makes sense.

When to go

Dry season and green season.

December to April is the dry season on the Pacific side and the high season for prices, with the Christmas, New Year and Easter weeks booking up first. Guanacaste in particular is reliably sunny.

May to November is the green season: mornings are often clear and rain tends to come in the afternoon. Prices drop and the landscape is at its greenest. September and October are usually the wettest months on the Pacific side, and some smaller lodges close for part of that time.

The Caribbean coast (Puerto Viejo, Cahuita) runs on a different pattern, and September and October are often among its drier months, which makes it a good choice when the Pacific is wettest.

Whatever the month, plan for rain gear in the cloud forest of Monteverde and around Arenal.

Where to stay

Pick a base, or a loop.

Guanacaste in the north-west has the country's biggest beach resorts and all-inclusives (around Papagayo, Tamarindo and the Gold Coast), close to Liberia's airport.

Arenal and La Fortuna are known for the volcano views, hot springs and waterfalls, a few hours' drive from either airport.

Manuel Antonio on the central Pacific coast combines beaches with the national park's rainforest and wildlife, about three hours from San José.

Monteverde (cloud forest), the Nicoya Peninsula (surf towns like Santa Teresa and Nosara) and the Osa Peninsula (remote lodges near Corcovado) take longer to reach. Our sister guide to the best hotels in Costa Rica compares regions in more depth.

Getting there

Two international airports, very different sides.

Juan Santamaría International (SJO), near San José, is the main airport and the better choice for Arenal, Manuel Antonio, Monteverde and the Caribbean coast. Daniel Oduber Quirós International (LIR) in Liberia is the gateway to Guanacaste, with many beach resorts under an hour away.

Distances look short on the map but drives take time on mountain roads, so allow a full travel day between regions. Many visitors rent a car; Costa Rican law requires third-party liability insurance on rentals, which often isn't in the headline price online, so compare the full cost. Check current entry requirements for your passport before you fly, including proof of onward travel.

Should you bundle?

For a beach stay, often. For a loop, rarely.

If you're flying into Liberia for a week at one Guanacaste resort, a flight and hotel package is worth pricing, because that's the kind of trip packages are built for. Compare the total with booking separately and check whether transfers are included.

For a multi-stop trip, say Arenal, Monteverde and Manuel Antonio, packages rarely fit: you'll usually do better booking flights separately and choosing each hotel for its location. Our guide to when bundling saves money explains how to tell.

Beyond the hotel

Wildlife, volcanoes and hot springs.

Guided walks at dawn are the best way to see sloths, monkeys and birds; national parks such as Manuel Antonio limit visitor numbers and sell entry online, so book ahead in high season. Around Arenal, hot springs and the La Fortuna waterfall fill a day easily. In Monteverde, hanging bridges and night walks show the cloud forest from different angles. From Guanacaste, Rincón de la Vieja National Park has volcanic mud pots and waterfalls within a day trip.

When is the best time to visit Costa Rica?

December to April is the dry season on the Pacific side and the most popular (and expensive) time. May to November is the green season, cheaper, with afternoon rain that is heaviest in September and October. The Caribbean coast is often drier in September and October.

Should I fly into San José or Liberia?

Fly into Liberia (LIR) for the Guanacaste beaches. Fly into San José (SJO) for Arenal, Manuel Antonio, Monteverde and the Caribbean coast. If you're doing a loop, you can fly into one and out of the other.

Is a Costa Rica flight and hotel package worth it?

Often for a single beach-resort stay in Guanacaste, rarely for a multi-stop trip. Compare the package total with booking separately and check whether airport transfers are included.

Do I need to rent a car in Costa Rica?

Not always. Shuttles and tours connect the main tourist areas, but a car gives more freedom. If you rent, include the mandatory third-party liability insurance when you compare prices, as it is often not shown in the first online quote.
